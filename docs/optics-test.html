Ghost Aquarium · Phase 1 optics test

Cardboard Ghost Test

Build a small cardboard box, put your phone under it, and see what a 27 mm ghost tank really looks like. It takes about 30 minutes and costs nothing. Do it before you buy the 1.5″ OLED.

How the trick works

The screen lies face-up. A clear sheet above it, tilted at 45°, reflects the screen's light toward your eye. Your brain traces that light straight back, so the fish seem to stand upright behind the sheet, in front of a black wall. Black pixels give off no light, so only the fish show.

What this test answers

SizeIs a 27 × 27 mm ghost big enough on your desk?
BrightnessCan you see it clearly in a dim room?
Double imageDoes the sheet's thickness show two overlapping copies?
MirroringDoes the word GHOST read correctly in the reflection?

If your phone has an OLED screen (most iPhones from the X onward, except the XR, 11 and SE), its black is true black, like the 1.5″ OLED. This is then a close preview of the real thing.

What you need

  • Thin cardboardA cereal box or shipping-box side, at least 160 × 110 mm
  • BlackBlack paper, or a black marker or matte paint for the inside faces. Avoid anything glossy
  • Clear sheetThe thinner and flatter the better: a CD case lid (about 1 mm), clear packaging plastic, or a transparency sheet. Picture-frame glass works but doubles more
  • ToolsRuler, scissors or a craft knife, tape
  • Your phoneOpen this page on it for the test screen

Build it

  1. Cut and fold the hood

    Cut one strip of cardboard 160 × 50 mm. Fold it at 50 mm and 110 mm into a U: side, back, side. Cut the sheet and the optional roof too.

  2. Black out the inside

    Color every inside face black, the back wall most of all. Any lit or shiny surface behind the sheet shows up in the reflection. Draw the two 45° guides after the black, with a pencil or a silver pen so you can still see them.

  3. Tape in the sheet

    • Peel any protective film off the sheet and wipe off fingerprints.
    • Rest the sheet on the two guides. Its bottom edge touches the bottom of the back wall, and its top edge reaches the top front corners.
    • Tape it along the outer edges only. Keep tape off the middle, where you look through.

    Trim the sheet if it bows between the walls. It has to lie flat, or the ghost warps.

  4. Add the roof (optional)

    Tape the roof on top, black side down. It blocks ceiling light from bouncing off the sheet and washing out the ghost.

  5. Set up your phone

    • Turn brightness all the way up.
    • Set Auto-Lock to Never for now, and turn on Do Not Disturb so no notification lights up the box.
    • Calibrate the size below once, then tap Start test screen.

  6. Cut the mask and lay the phone down

    Cut black paper the size of your phone, with a 27 × 27 mm square window in the middle. Lay the phone face-up with its camera end toward you. Tap Align, then lay the paper so the window sits exactly over the gray square outline. Tape the corners.

    The mask hides the rest of the screen, including the browser bar, so only a 27 mm square of light reaches the sheet. That's exactly the size of the OLED's visible area.

  7. Place the hood

    Set the hood over the window with the open side toward you. Leave about 5 mm between the back wall and the window's far edge, and center the window between the side walls.

  8. Look

    Dim the room. Lower your head until your eye is level with the middle of the sheet, about 30–50 cm away, and look through the open front. The fish should hang in the air at the back wall. Tap Text check and make sure GHOST reads correctly.

If something looks wrong

What you seeFix
GHOST reads backwardsTap Mirror to switch it.
Tank is upside down (sand on top)Turn the phone around so the camera end points the other way, then re-check the text.
Two overlapping copies of each fishThe sheet is too thick. Try thinner plastic. The real build uses 0.76 mm PETG.
Ghost is faintDarken the room, add the roof, and make sure brightness is at maximum.
A gray square around the fishYour phone has an LCD, which can't show true black. This is the glow the OLED removes, so it isn't a fail.
Top or bottom of the tank is cut offSlide the hood so the whole window sits between the back wall and the front edge.
You see the phone, not a ghostYour eye is too high. Look straight through the front at sheet height.

Report back

Note four things: whether 27 mm feels big enough, how bright it is in a dim room, whether you see a double image, and which Mirror setting read correctly. That confirms or overturns the display decision before ordering parts.
